Overview

Artificial intelligence is increasingly capable of performing activities that have traditionally required human judgment, expertise, and coordination. AI systems can analyze information, generate business deliverables, use software tools, make decisions, and execute increasingly complex tasks.

But how do we know what these systems are actually capable of doing?

AI benchmarks provide a way to evaluate and compare AI systems by testing their performance on defined tasks. Some benchmarks assess reasoning or problem-solving, while others evaluate practical capabilities such as using software, completing knowledge-work assignments, coordinating activities, or operating as autonomous agents.

For organizations, the important question is not simply how well an AI system performs on a benchmark. It is what that performance tells us about the system's ability to perform useful work within a business process.

In this assignment, your team will investigate an AI benchmark, explain its methodology and results, and evaluate its implications for Business Process Management (BPM), process automation, and agentic AI.

Central question: What does this benchmark reveal about AI's ability to perform, coordinate, or automate work within business processes?

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the purpose, design, and evaluation methodology of an AI benchmark.
  2. Interpret benchmark results and identify important AI capabilities and limitations.
  3. Connect benchmark tasks to activities, workflows, and end-to-end business processes.
  4. Evaluate implications for process automation, agentic workflows, and human–AI collaboration.
  5. Critically assess whether benchmark performance provides convincing evidence of readiness for real-world business deployment.

Assignment Requirements

Your team will deliver a 15-minute presentation examining an assigned or approved AI benchmark.

Your presentation should explain what the benchmark measures, how it works, what its results reveal, and why those results matter for the design and management of business processes.

The objective is not to provide an exhaustive technical analysis or simply compare AI model rankings. Your task is to translate benchmark findings into meaningful insights about the evolving capabilities of AI in business operations.

Suggested Presentation Arc

The following structure is recommended. You may adapt the sequence to suit your benchmark, but all six areas should be addressed.

1. Why Does This Benchmark Matter? 2 minutes

Introduce the benchmark by establishing the capability or problem it is designed to evaluate.

  • What aspect of AI capability is being tested?
  • Why is this capability difficult or important?
  • What kinds of activities might benefit from this capability?
  • Why should organizations care about advances in this area?

Objective: Establish the significance of the benchmark and motivate the audience to understand its results.

2. How Does the Benchmark Work? 3 minutes

Explain what AI systems are required to do and how their performance is evaluated.

  • Who developed the benchmark, and what was its purpose?
  • What types of tasks, problems, or scenarios are included?
  • What inputs, information, tools, or resources are available to the AI?
  • What outputs, actions, or deliverables are expected?
  • How is performance evaluated and scored?

Include at least one specific example of a benchmark task. Explain what the AI must accomplish and what constitutes successful performance. For agent benchmarks, distinguish between what the agent is expected to accomplish and the tools or actions it is permitted to use.

Objective: Help the audience understand what the benchmark actually tests and how its results are produced.

3. What Do the Results Reveal? 3 minutes

Present and interpret the benchmark's findings.

  • How well do current AI systems perform?
  • Which models or approaches perform particularly well?
  • What types of tasks are relatively easy or difficult?
  • Is there evidence of improving performance over time?
  • How does AI performance compare with human performance, where meaningful comparisons are available?

Use a chart, table, or other visual to communicate the results. Go beyond reporting scores: explain what those scores mean in terms of AI capabilities and limitations. Pay attention to differences in evaluation conditions, including available tools, task constraints, model versions, and scoring methods.

Objective: Develop an evidence-based understanding of what AI systems can and cannot currently accomplish.

4. What Are the Implications for Business Processes? 4 minutes

This is the central analytical component of the presentation. Connect the benchmark to BPM, BPMN, process mining, workflow automation, and AI agents.

Task execution

AI performs an individual activity, such as analyzing a document, evaluating a transaction, or preparing a report.

Workflow execution

AI coordinates multiple related activities, uses tools, manages dependencies, and responds to intermediate results or exceptions.

End-to-end process execution

AI manages a complete process involving multiple activities, decisions, systems, participants, controls, and outcomes.

  • Which of these levels does the benchmark actually evaluate?
  • What business activities or processes could benefit from the demonstrated capabilities?
  • How might AI be incorporated into an existing business workflow?
  • Would AI act autonomously, assist human workers, or operate under human supervision?
  • What changes might be required in process design, controls, or exception handling?

Use a specific business example. Where useful, a simplified BPMN diagram can show where AI participates in a process and how its work interacts with other activities.

Objective: Translate benchmark performance into practical implications for business process design, execution, and automation.

5. Critical Assessment: Is the Capability Ready for Business Use? 2 minutes

Evaluate how convincingly the benchmark demonstrates readiness for deployment.

  • How realistic are the tasks compared with actual business work?
  • Does the benchmark evaluate isolated activities or sustained, multi-step execution?
  • Does it capture reliability, error recovery, cost, execution time, and operational constraints?
  • What are the consequences of errors in a real business process?
  • What additional evidence would an organization need before deploying the capability?

Distinguish between demonstrating a capability under benchmark conditions and operating it reliably within a business process.

Assess whether the demonstrated capability is best suited for autonomous execution, human-supervised execution, or further experimentation. The answer may depend on the business context and consequences of failure.

Objective: Demonstrate critical judgment about benchmark results and real-world business readiness.

6. Final Takeaway 1 minute

  1. What is the most important AI capability evaluated by this benchmark?
  2. What do the results suggest about the current maturity of that capability?
  3. What is the most significant implication for the future of business process management?

End with one clear insight that your classmates should remember.

Objective: Leave the audience with a meaningful conclusion about AI capabilities and business processes.

Research and Evidence

Support your presentation with credible sources, including the benchmark's original publication or documentation and available evaluation results. Where appropriate, consult independent analyses, research papers, or technical reports to understand its methodology and limitations.

Clearly distinguish reported results, AI developers' claims, and your team's interpretations. Cite important factual claims and include a reference slide.

Presentation Guidelines

  • Duration: 15 minutes.
  • Format: Team presentation supported by slides.
  • Visual communication: Use concrete examples, benchmark results, charts, and diagrams where they improve understanding.
  • Business relevance: Explicitly connect findings to business activities, process automation, workflow design, and AI agents.
  • Technical depth: Explain enough to support informed discussion without unnecessary complexity.
  • Participation: All team members should contribute meaningfully.

A BPMN diagram is encouraged when it helps illustrate business implications, but it is not mandatory.

Final Perspective

AI benchmarks provide valuable evidence about the capabilities of emerging AI systems. However, performing well on a benchmark does not necessarily mean that an AI system can reliably perform the same work in a real business environment.

Business processes involve dependencies, exceptions, coordination, controls, and organizational consequences that may not be captured by benchmark evaluations.

As AI advances, organizations will increasingly need to determine not only what AI can do, but also where, when, and under what conditions it should be trusted to perform work.

Your presentation should help us understand that distinction and what it means for the future of business process management.
